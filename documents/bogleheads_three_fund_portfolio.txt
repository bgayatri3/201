# Three-Fund Portfolio

## What Is a Three-Fund Portfolio?

A three-fund portfolio is a simple investing strategy that uses three broad-market index funds:

1. Domestic Stock Market Fund
2. International Stock Market Fund
3. Total Bond Market Fund

The goal is to create a diversified, low-cost portfolio that is easy to manage and rebalance.

## Core Components

### Domestic Stocks

Provides exposure to the U.S. stock market through a total market index fund.

### International Stocks

Provides exposure to companies outside the United States and increases diversification.

### Bonds

Provides stability and helps reduce portfolio volatility.

## Establishing a Three-Fund Portfolio

Building a three-fund portfolio involves three major decisions:

### 1. Choose Your Asset Allocation

Determine:

- What percentage of your portfolio should be invested in stocks versus bonds.
- What percentage of your stock allocation should be domestic versus international.

Common considerations:

- Risk tolerance
- Age
- Retirement timeline
- Financial goals

### 2. Choose Asset Location

Place investments in the most tax-efficient accounts.

General guidelines:

- Bonds are often held in tax-advantaged accounts.
- International funds may be beneficial in taxable accounts.
- Domestic stock funds can fill remaining space.

### 3. Choose Low-Cost Funds

The Boglehead philosophy emphasizes:

- Low expense ratios
- Broad diversification
- Total market index funds
- Long-term investing

## Common Vanguard Three-Fund Portfolio

Typical fund selections include:

### Vanguard Total Stock Market Index Fund (VTSAX)

Provides exposure to the entire U.S. stock market.

### Vanguard Total International Stock Index Fund (VTIAX)

Provides exposure to international markets.

### Vanguard Total Bond Market Index Fund (VBTLX)

Provides exposure to investment-grade U.S. bonds.

## Example Allocation

A three-fund portfolio might consist of:

- 42% U.S. Stocks
- 18% International Stocks
- 40% Bonds

The exact allocation depends on the investor's goals and risk tolerance.

## Benefits of a Three-Fund Portfolio

### Simplicity

Only three funds are required.

### Diversification

Provides exposure to thousands of stocks and bonds worldwide.

### Low Cost

Index funds generally have very low expense ratios.

### Easy Maintenance

Requires only occasional rebalancing.

### Strong Historical Performance

Many investors believe a simple diversified portfolio outperforms the majority of actively managed portfolios over long periods.

## Alternative Fund Providers

A Boglehead-style portfolio does not require Vanguard funds.

Investors can use:

- Fidelity index funds
- Schwab index funds
- Other low-cost index funds and ETFs

The key principle is broad diversification and low expenses.

## International Allocation Considerations

There is ongoing debate about how much international exposure investors should hold.

Common recommendations include:

- 20%–40% of stock allocation in international stocks.
- Global market-weight allocations.
- Personal preference based on risk tolerance.

## Bond Allocation Considerations

Bonds are included to:

- Reduce volatility.
- Provide stability during market downturns.
- Improve risk-adjusted returns.

Some investors also consider:

- International bonds
- Certificates of Deposit (CDs)
- Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities (TIPS)

## Three-Fund Portfolio vs. Target Retirement Funds

Target Retirement Funds offer a similar strategy in a single fund.

### Advantages of a Three-Fund Portfolio

- Greater control over allocation.
- Better tax efficiency.
- Lower costs in some cases.
- More flexibility.

### Advantages of Target Retirement Funds

- Simplicity.
- Automatic rebalancing.
- No need to manage allocations manually.

## Four-Fund Portfolio Variation

Some investors add:

### International Bond Fund

Creating a four-fund portfolio consisting of:

1. U.S. Stocks
2. International Stocks
3. U.S. Bonds
4. International Bonds

This approach is used in many Vanguard Target Retirement Funds.

## Key Takeaways

- A three-fund portfolio uses domestic stocks, international stocks, and bonds.
- It emphasizes simplicity, diversification, and low costs.
- Asset allocation is more important than selecting individual funds.
- Broad-market index funds are commonly recommended.
- Rebalancing periodically helps maintain the desired allocation.
- Many investors view a three-fund portfolio as sufficient for achieving long-term financial goals.
